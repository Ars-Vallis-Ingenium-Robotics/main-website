Remotely Operated Submersible for Innovation and Exploration. Modular, compact, under the 18 kg weight limit, and built around the pressure hull MATE provided.

## spec
kicker: Spec sheet
title: The short version.

- Propulsion: 8 × Blue Robotics T200, vectored, 6-DOF
- Frame: Aluminum T-slot + 3D-printed PETG, TPU feet, PVC legs
- Power: 48 V / 30 A tether, buck converters to 12 V and 5 V
- Compute: Raspberry Pi 4B, Ethernet to a custom surface GUI
- Vision: Front, rear and downward cameras; IMU for stability
- Tools: Rotating claw (16:1 gearbox), four harpoons, eDNA sampling
- Mass: Under 18 kg

## chapters

### Five frames before the final one
Version 0 was a foamboard octagon. Version 1 used PVC pipe so we could test layouts before cutting any aluminum. The final frame uses T-slot rail for modularity, with 3D-printed PETG parts at 35% infill and five walls. PETG won our decision matrix because it doesn't warp in Central Valley heat the way PLA does.

### Stacked thrusters, six degrees of freedom
Eight Blue Robotics T200 thrusters in stacked mounts give full 6-DOF control and keep thruster wash off the hull. The layout came from studying MATE's top ten product-demonstration teams.

### Harpoons and a rotating claw
Four rigid harpoons grab samples on a drive-by; on the qualification run ROSIE surfaced a basket star, the eDNA sensor, a cap and a coral sample at once. The claw rotates through two 4:1 planetary gearboxes (16:1 total) on plastic bushings that won't rust.

### Jigs that saved hours
Loose T-nuts in the rail turned every rebuild into a fight. Thin printed jigs hold them in place, cutting thruster and manipulator setup from about two hours to 30 minutes.
